# Executive Summary

A payroll remediation review was conducted across 100 employees, 105 employment contracts, and more than 45,000 timesheet records. The analysis examined workforce composition, compensation structures, minimum wage compliance, overtime obligations, allowances, bonuses, leave utilisation, and payroll risk exposure.

Key findings indicate a workforce heavily reliant on Casual and Fixed-Term employment arrangements, significant complexity in payroll calculations due to multiple penalty and loading rules, and substantial expenditure on incentive-based compensation. The dashboard provides visibility into potential compliance risks and supports payroll remediation activities through employee-level analysis.

# 3. Payroll Compliance & Remediation Exposure

## Minimum Wage Analysis

The review incorporated Australian minimum wage schedules across multiple financial years.

| Financial Year | Permanent Rate | Casual Rate |
| -------------- | -------------: | ----------: |
| 2019–20        |         $19.49 |      $24.36 |
| 2020–21        |         $19.84 |      $24.80 |
| 2021–22        |         $20.33 |      $25.41 |
| 2022–23        |         $21.38 |      $26.73 |

### Insights

* Employee pay rates range from $20.08 to $49.87 per hour.
* Employees paid near the lower end of the pay range require ongoing review whenever minimum wage increases occur.
* Casual employees present higher compliance risk because both minimum wage requirements and casual loading must be applied correctly.
* Historical wage increases create additional remediation risk where employee pay rates have not been updated promptly.

### Recommendation

Prioritise employees with pay rates closest to minimum wage thresholds for compliance review.

# 4. Overtime, Penalties & Payroll Complexity

The payroll framework includes multiple pay adjustments:

| Rate Type                | Multiplier |
| ------------------------ | ---------: |
| Overtime (First 2 Hours) |       1.5× |
| Overtime (After 2 Hours) |       2.0× |
| Sunday Overtime          |       2.5× |
| Sunday Penalty           |       2.0× |
| Night Shift Penalty      |      1.25× |
| Casual Loading           |       +25% |

### Insights

* Six separate loading and penalty calculations are applied across payroll processing.
* Complex pay structures significantly increase the risk of payroll errors.
* Incorrect application of overtime multipliers or penalty rates is a common source of underpayment claims.
* Casual employees face the highest exposure due to additional loading requirements.

### Recommendation

Implement automated validation checks for overtime, Sunday shifts, night work, and casual loading calculations.

# 9. Payroll Remediation Recommendations

## Immediate Priorities

### 1. Underpayment Audit

Identify employees whose pay rates fall below applicable minimum wage requirements, including casual loading obligations.

### 2. Junior Pay Rate Validation

Review employee age progression against junior pay rate schedules to ensure automatic rate increases have been applied correctly.

### 3. Overtime Verification

Compare rostered and actual hours to identify unpaid overtime or incorrectly applied penalty rates.

### 4. Sunday & Night Shift Review

Validate all Sunday and night-shift payments against applicable payroll rules.

### 5. TOIL Reconciliation

Confirm Time-Off-In-Lieu balances correctly offset overtime liabilities.

### 6. Contract Compliance Review

Verify no payroll payments continue after contract expiry or employee termination dates.

# Conclusion

The analysis identified several areas requiring ongoing payroll monitoring, particularly minimum wage compliance, overtime calculations, casual loading application, and contract management. The dashboard provides a centralised reporting solution that improves visibility into payroll risks and supports remediation efforts through detailed employee-level analysis.
